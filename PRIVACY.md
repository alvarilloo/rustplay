# RustPlay privacy policy

_Last updated: 2026-11-15_

RustPlay is a free, open-source AirPlay sender and receiver for Windows. This policy explains what it does with your data.

## What RustPlay collects

**Nothing.** RustPlay has no accounts, no analytics, no telemetry and no advertising. It does not send any personal data to the developer or to third parties.

## Network use

- **Local network only for streaming.** Screen, audio, video and photos travel directly between your PC and your Apple devices or TV over your local network (AirPlay). They are not sent to any server.
- **Device discovery.** RustPlay announces itself and looks for AirPlay devices on your network (Bonjour/mDNS).
- **Update check.** The classic installer version may contact `api.github.com` and `github.com` to check for new releases and download them. No identifier is sent beyond what a normal HTTPS request contains. The Microsoft Store version is updated by the Store.

## Data stored on your PC

RustPlay stores the following locally, in your user profile, and never uploads it:

- Settings, the receiver name and the list of devices paired with RustPlay (pairing keys).
- A history of connections, recordings and errors (you can clear it in the app).
- Log files, which include local IP addresses of connected devices.
- Recordings and screenshots you choose to make, in the folders you choose.

Uninstalling the app and deleting its data folder removes all of it.

## Screen and audio access

RustPlay captures your screen and system audio only when you start sending to a device, and receives screens, audio, videos and photos from other devices only when the receiver is turned on and a device connects. You can optionally require a 4-digit code before a device can connect.

## Children

RustPlay does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
